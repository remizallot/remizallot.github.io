Our paper ["Characterization of putatively lytic bacteriophages able to infect *Xanthomonas citri* subsp. *citri* and identification of novel putative exopolysaccharide depolymerases"](/publication/2026-09-15-Characterization-of-putatively-lytic-bacteriophages-able-to-infect-Xanthomonas-citri-subsp-citri-and-identification-of-novel-putative-exopolysaccharide-depolymerases) is out in *Microbial Genomics*.

Together with colleagues in Brazil, the US and the UK, we isolated and sequenced 72 bacteriophages that infect *Xanthomonas citri*, the bacterium that causes Asiatic citrus canker. Almost all of them are closely related, like the bacterial population they infect. Using structure-based annotation (HHsearch and AlphaFold), we found conserved tail-fibre proteins that may be depolymerases, able to break down the bacterium's xanthan gum capsule. In greenhouse trials, one phage cut canker lesions by 60%. This makes phages a promising, sustainable alternative to copper sprays.
